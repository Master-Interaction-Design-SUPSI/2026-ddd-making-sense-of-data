---
title: Dust
authors: Ginevra Terenghi
---

What does dust reveal about the Bolivian landscape? Materials can act as records of phenomena, carrying traces of the processes and interactions through which they have been transformed. This exploration draws on [[autographic visualisation|https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2019.2934788]], which considers physical traces and material indicators as forms of data (Offenhuber, 2019), [[data physicalisation|https://doi.org/10.1145/3617366]], where physical and material properties become part of how data are represented and interpreted (Hornecker et al., 2023), and Schön’s [[reflective conversation with materials|https://search.worldcat.org/title/223087924]] (1983). Building on these perspectives and my previous research into material engagement and data physicalisation, I ask: How can the material conditions of a landscape participate in producing its representation? My experiments work with processes already occurring within landscapes—accumulation, stratification, temperature, vibration and deposition—as means of recording and communication. In Materia Profonda, geological stratification informed an activity in which sands of different colours and granularities accumulated to materialise experiences of time. [[See the material experiment|cariola-materia-profonda.png]] Extending this approach to direct encounters with landscapes, thermal imaging translates environmental temperature into visible differences across Icelandic glacial and volcanic formations. [[Thermal trace of a geothermal landscape|iceland-gyser.png]] [[Thermal trace of ice|iceland-iceberg.png]] Terrain is recorded differently through sound: vibrations generated by off-road routes travel [[through vehicle and body |aaa_ID-1.mp4]], modulating a continuous vocal sound and producing [[route-specific sound traces|aaa_ID-unknown.mp3]] through physical contact with the ground. In Bolivia, adhesive surfaces become recording devices: dust, salt and other particles attach through contact, deposition and accumulation, producing material samples whose colour, granularity and distribution vary between territories. [[Dust trace 1|bolivia-dust-01.png]] [[Dust trace 2|bolivia-dust-02.png]] [[Dust trace 3|bolivia-dust-03.png]] Across these experiments, {crimson|the environment participates in producing its own trace}: temperature becomes image, terrain becomes sound, and particles become texture. These material encounters offer situated and sensory ways of making differences between landscapes perceptible.
