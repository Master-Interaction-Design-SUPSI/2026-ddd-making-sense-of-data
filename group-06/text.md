---
title: What Does a Data Centre Sound Like?
authors: Noyan and Alireza
---

*What does an AI data centre sound like, and who gets to describe it?* The noise is not abstract. It comes from **cooling systems**, and secondarily from the turbines and generators that power the site. We collected [107 published sources](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1z5Xn5UeWda6527Jl30bCQTBp5d59W9P35E3xEjl-Weo/edit?gid=516013045#gid=516013045) from 2018 to 2026 (news stories, reports, reviews and lawsuits) and coded each one by the machine it names, the pitch it gives the sound, the words it uses and who is speaking.

Description often stops short. 36 sources never say which machine makes the noise, and 57 never say whether it is high or low. 23 call it only {#d9480f|noise} or "loud" without saying what it sounds like. When a source does find a word, it is most often **hum** (53 sources), then tone (18), whine (15) and buzz (13). Low words outnumber high ones: 26 sources describe a low sound, 15 a high one.

When words fail, people compare. 21 sources say it sounds *like an aircraft* and 12 *like a vacuum*. In Dowagiac, Michigan, a lawsuit blames the cooling, neighbours call it a high-pitched hum, and one resident says it was like having a vacuum in her living room. The same sound gets different words depending on who speaks: residents (30 sources) compare it to things they know, while institutions (15) classify it as a {#1c5d99|tone} or a frequency.

The pitch moves over time. Early sources, many about one site in Chandler, Arizona, call the sound a high whine; later ones about large AI campuses describe a low hum, a rumble and finally infrasound. [[a measuring gap|Noise rules measure loudness in dB(A), a weighting that discounts low frequencies, the range people complain about most.|type=note;color=green]]

Our dataset has limits. 94 of the 107 sources were read against the original page; 13 could not be opened or only by headline, and keep their earlier coding. Each source counts once per sound word it uses, so the word counts add up to more than 107. Several sources repeat the same story, and most are American.
