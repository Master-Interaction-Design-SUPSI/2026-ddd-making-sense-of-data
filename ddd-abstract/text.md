Digital technologies are often perceived as immaterial, yet they depend on natural resources [[media 1|media/1.png]], physical infrastructures [[media 2|media/2.png]], and human labour [[media 3|media/3.png]] [[Parikka, 2015|A Geology of Media|type=bibliography]]. Artificial intelligence builds upon these foundations, introducing additional demands through large-scale computation, extensive data processing, and increasingly complex models [[Crawford, 2021|Atlas of AI|type=bibliography]]. These activities consume electricity, require cooling systems that may use substantial amounts of water [[media 4|media/4.mp4]], and contribute to environmental impacts [[media 5|media/5.mp3]] across hardware production [[media 6|media/6.png]], operation, and disposal [[Aczel et al., 2026|Environmental cost of AI’s energy use: Carbon, water and land footprints|type=bibliography]]. Investigating these material dependencies involves two complementary approaches: {crimson|revealing} the resources [[media 7|media/7.png]] [[media 8|media/8.png]], infrastructures [[media 8|media/8.png]] and labour behind AI systems, and {green|tracking} [[media 9|media/9.png]] [[media 10|media/10.png]] their material traces across extraction, production, use, and disposal. Both approaches can be applied across four interconnected dimensions, each opening different questions about AI’s materialities: 1) Geology and ecology {crimson|*✶ What does AI extract and consume?*} Minerals [[media 11|media/11.png]], land [[media 12|media/12.mp4]], energy, and water, with uneven environmental impacts across territories [[Nicoletti et al., 2025|The AI Boom Is Draining Water From the Areas That Need It Most|type=bibliography]]. 2) Infrastructure and computation {crimson|*✶ Where and how is AI computation performed?*} Data centres, factories, cables, and processors reveal its geographical organisation [[media 13|media/13.png]] [[Coletta et al., 2022|Mapping the Clouds: The Matter of Data Centers|type=bibliography]]. 3) Human labour {crimson|*✶ Whose work makes AI possible?*} [[media 14|media/14.png]] Mining, manufacturing, data annotation [[media 15|media/15.png]] [[media 16|media/16.png]], moderation, maintenance, and disposal sustain automated systems [[Gray & Suri, 2019|Ghost Work: How to Stop Silicon Valley from Building a New Global Underclass|type=bibliography]]. 4) Embodiment {crimson|*✶ How does AI interact with bodies and environments?*} Sensors, cameras [[media 17|media/17.png]], wearables, robots [[media 18|media/18.png]], and connected objects embed algorithmic processes into everyday life [[Greenfield, 2017|Radical Technologies: The Design of Everyday Life|type=bibliography]]. Each investigation focuses on a specific aspect of AI’s materialities, explored through the collection and analysis of data. The findings are presented through a data visualisation and a multimodal abstract designed to present the topic and expand its interpretation.
